## 2. 記述設問（英文・語数検算済み）

### Short background — Business lead（max 120 words）

```text
Serial entrepreneur with five company foundings, focused on taking emerging technologies to market across Japan, Southeast Asia and North America. Founded and scaled a Web3 solutions company in Malaysia to an 80-person multinational team (2017–2020), then led a Los Angeles-based satellite-data venture as CEO (2020–2022), raising a cumulative JPY 450 million in seed funding across ventures. Since 2023 has led regional business development in Chiba Prefecture with local governments, and currently serves as a Regional Revitalization Entrepreneur for Shirako Town. Full-time on PhaseShift since April 2026: customer development with domestic iodine producers, public-funding applications (JST, NEDO, ERCA) and IP and contract strategy with Doshisha University. Completed the University of Tokyo Matsuo-Iwasawa Lab AI Management Course and NEDO SSA.
```

### Briefly describe your technology and its current stage（max 240 words）

```text
PhaseShift recovers iodine from dilute aqueous streams that today's commercial processes leave behind. Japan produces iodine from natural-gas brine by blow-out and ion-exchange; from published plant data we estimate that roughly one-third of the iodine (7–12 ppm) leaves with the discharged brine, because neither method is economical there.

Our core material is a temperature-responsive polymer developed at Doshisha University. It selectively binds iodine in water and phase-separates on a modest temperature change, concentrating the iodine; a temperature swing then releases it, so desorption is designed to need no reducing agents or solvents. The unit is a side-stream, fixed-bed retrofit beside the customer's existing plant that does not stop production; low-grade waste heat is the design heat source, not yet demonstrated.

Current stage: laboratory proof-of-concept, batch scale. In pure water at 7 ppm, 96.8% of iodine was captured (UV-vis, supernatant basis); in a 7 ppm model brine wastewater, supernatant iodine fell below the detection limit; in hexane the polymer captured 91.4% versus 73.9% for conventional PVP. These are capture figures. Recovery after desorption on real wastewater is not yet measured.

Known gaps: about 30% polymer loss per cycle in the current liquid-liquid separation (a solid-precipitating copolymer is under test), one-cycle reuse at 79.4% with cause unresolved, and no continuous-flow data. Two major domestic producers have agreed to supply real wastewater; a TRL4 assessment on those samples is scheduled for late September 2026, and a 100 L/day bench unit for 2027.
```

### Who are your target customers / markets?（max 180 words）

```text
Initial hypothesis, in two phases.

Phase 1, Japan (beachhead): iodine producers on the Boso gas field in Chiba Prefecture, which supplies roughly 80% of Japan's output; Japan supplies about 30% of the world's iodine. Brine pumping is capped by ground-subsidence agreements, so producers cannot grow by pumping more; recovering iodine from their own discharge stream is one of the few remaining ways to increase output. We are in technical discussions with two major producers (Producer A, under NDA, and Producer B), both of which have agreed to provide real wastewater samples, and Chiba Prefecture has arranged a meeting with a third producer in mid-September. Customers own the unit; we supply the polymer and charge a per-kilogram process fee on recovered iodine.

Phase 2, North America (conditional on domestic results): water-midstream and saltwater-disposal operators in Oklahoma's Anadarko Basin whose produced water is not connected to existing iodine plants, plus the low-concentration streams downstream of those plants. Below roughly 50–100 ppm, iodine value is less than disposal cost, so a low-CAPEX retrofit is the only viable form.
```

### Why go global, and which geographies would you target first?（max 180 words）

```text
Japan is our proving ground, not our ceiling. Domestic brine flow is fixed by regulation, so the domestic opportunity is bounded: we estimate 700–1,000 t/yr of iodine is currently uncaptured nationwide. The same structure exists at larger scale in US oil-and-gas produced water. Iofina operates eight iodine plants in the Anadarko Basin, proving that iodine recovery from produced water is a real business there; by our estimate those plants take water at 59–108 ppm and leave residual streams comparable to or richer than Japan's 7 ppm tails. The unit economics we are validating in Japan, a side-stream retrofit owned by the water operator, transfer directly: one Japanese site (about 13,000 bbl/day) matches the lower end of a US plant's intake.

First geography: Oklahoma (Anadarko Basin), then the Permian Basin in Texas. Sequence: establish unit economics and operating data in Japan (2026–2028), then run a 12-week North American feasibility study once defined gate conditions are met. The key technical unknown is polymer behavior at TDS above 100,000 mg/L, to be tested on real US samples.
```

### Known competitors or alternatives（max 120 words）

```text
Incumbent processes: blow-out and ion-exchange, used by all Japanese producers, are economical only at higher concentrations. Neither has published performance data in the 7–12 ppm range, and nor have we, so we do not claim the space is empty. Iofina (USA): vertically integrated producer with eight Oklahoma plants using its IOsorb resin on 59–108 ppm produced water; we position downstream of such plants, not against them, but Iofina could move to lower concentrations. Patents: WO2025258648A1 (a Japanese university with a domestic producer) targets 10 ppm and above with reducing-agent desorption; ours uses temperature swing. Reverse-osmosis membranes lose rejection below about 15 ppm. The practical alternative is doing nothing and discharging.
```

### What does your team most want to get from this program?（max 180 words）

```text
1. US customer discovery. Structured introductions to water-midstream and saltwater-disposal operators and produced-water experts in Oklahoma and Texas, to test whether low-concentration, unconnected streams exist at meaningful volume. That question decides our Phase 2.
2. Business-model stress test. Mentors who have commercialized separation or water technologies to challenge our asset-light model (customer-owned unit, consumables, per-kg process fee) for the US, including offtake for concentrated iodine.
3. IP and regulatory strategy. Guidance on US and PCT filing for our October 2026 application patent, freedom to operate versus IOsorb-type resins, and produced-water handling rules.
4. Investor readiness. Preparation for a pre-seed and seed round (two-stage J-KISS planned), with feedback from deep-tech and climate investors on the milestones that make a Japan-first resource-recovery venture fundable globally.
5. Third-party validation partners. Introductions to US universities or labs able to test our polymer on high-salinity samples.
We bring real wastewater data from Japanese producers and a defined gate framework, so the sprint can end with a go/no-go on North America.
```
